Go through my whole Photos library with Apple Bridge and organize it into albums that make sense for my life. Don't use a fixed list of categories. Look at what's actually there and pick albums that fit me.

How to work:

1. Get the lay of the land first. List my existing albums, then use photos.places and searches by year and month to see where and when I take photos. Look for clusters: a burst of photos somewhere away from home is probably a trip, the same place over and over is probably home, work or school.
2. Look at the photos themselves to understand them. Use contact sheets of about 20 photos per sample, spread across each cluster, rather than every single photo. Open a photo larger only when you can't tell what it is.
3. Before you create anything, show me a plan: each album you want to make, its name, roughly how many photos, and a sentence on how you decided what goes in it. Wait for my ok.
4. Then build it. Create each album, add photos in batches, and read the album back to check the count.

What good albums look like:

- Trips named by place and date, like "Lisbon, May 2025". Split separate visits to the same place.
- Events like weddings, birthdays, graduations and holidays, named by what and when.
- People I clearly photograph a lot, named by how they show up, like "Mom" if it's obvious from context or "Friend with red hair" if not. Ask me to name them instead of guessing.
- Ongoing themes that show up across years, like pets, food, sports, concerts, nature, cars or projects, but only if there are enough photos to be worth an album.
- Practical stuff like screenshots, receipts, documents, whiteboards and memes, so they're out of the way.
- Only go as fine-grained as the library deserves. Ten good albums beat a hundred thin ones.

Rules:

- Never delete, hide or edit a photo. Only create albums and add photos to them.
- Don't touch my existing albums or rename them. If one already covers something, tell me instead of making a duplicate.
- A photo can be in more than one album.
- If you're unsure where something goes, leave it out and list it at the end.
- Keep what you see private. Don't describe people's faces or private documents back to me in detail.

Update me through the process so I can know what you are doing.

When you're done, give me a short summary: the albums you made, how many photos are in each, and anything you weren't sure about.
